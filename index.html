Featured graduate research · 2025–2026

Throwing Shade: How Trees Help Cities Beat the Heat

M.S. Directed Research · California State University, Dominguez Hills

A comparative, parcel-scale urban microclimate study in Carson, California, examining vegetated and minimally vegetated environments near the Royal Boulevard Landfill.

Study design & field monitoring

Compared two HOBO air-temperature monitoring locations on one residential property: a vegetated street-facing area and a minimally vegetated landfill-adjacent area. Used repeated observations from October through January.

Data preparation & analysis

Cleaned temperature observations, checked timestamps and outliers, calculated mean, minimum, maximum, and ranges, and compared temporal patterns and daily site differences. Consulted nearby NOAA weather-station observations for broader context.

Selected findings

Mean air temperature was 62.88°F at the vegetated location versus 57.61°F at the landfill-adjacent location; the manuscript reports a mean daily difference of +5.24°F. Separately, cement surface temperatures averaged 59.56°F versus 54.12°F for vegetated surfaces.

Interpretation & limitations

The vegetated site was warmer in air-temperature observations, contrary to the initial hypothesis, while vegetated surfaces were cooler. The manuscript discusses airflow and local site configuration as possible explanations and identifies the single-property study design as a limitation. These are observational comparisons, not evidence of a causal cooling effect.

Independent research workflow

Independently planned and executed the study, deployed HOBO sensors, transferred and organized monitoring records in Excel, cleaned and quantified observations, created figures, interpreted findings, and presented results. Used R for coding and visualizations when Excel alone could not support the intended analysis.

Research tools & experience

HOBO environmental monitoring, Excel, R, Python, and ArcGIS. Additional field observations included vegetation and multilayered canopy conditions. A handheld environmental meter was also used; its model and measured variables are being verified before listing instrument-specific methods.

Broader research output: Across academic and community research projects, worked with approximately 10 datasets, prepared 6 reports, and delivered 18 presentations (self-reported approximate totals).

Scientific communication & recognition

Prepared a literature synthesis, methods, figures, discussion, and research presentation. Awarded First Place at the CSUDH Student Research Conference and a Sustainability Engagement Award.
